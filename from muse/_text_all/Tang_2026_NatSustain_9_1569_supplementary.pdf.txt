Supplementary Fig. 4 | The sensitivity test results at 2 km and 3 km spatial resolutions. a, 
The spatial comparison between the model predictions and the ground-truth benchmarks. The data 
was normalized to highlight the model’s consistency in capturing mobility patterns. b, The 
improvements in prediction performance with growing generations at the 2 km grid scale. In the 
simulations for both Zhengzhou and Shenzhen, the model’s accuracy exhibited variability and 
growth over the initial 50 generations, eventually stabilized at a higher and consistent level. c, The 
correlation between modeled and actual inflow volumes. At the 2 km resolution, the scatter points 
show a wider spread around the diagonal line, indicating a slight reduction in model fit. The 
residual analyses at 2 km resolution for Zhengzhou (d) and Shenzhen (e) show approximately 
symmetric distributions centered around zero, as illustrated by the histograms and kernel density 
estimation (KDE) curves. The Q–Q plots further indicate that Shenzhen’s residuals closely follow 
a normal distribution (𝑅𝑅2 = 0.93), while Zhengzhou exhibits more substantial deviation (𝑅𝑅2 = 
0.60). At the 3 km grid scale, the predictive capability demonstrates a similar trend, with an initial 
increase followed by stabilization over the generation process (g). However, larger deviations are 
observed compared to the other two resolutions (f), and the ability to capture the granularity of 
hotspot details declines (h). As expected, a coarser grid scale (2 km or 3 km spatial resolution) 
slightly reduces the model’s performance with respect to inflow volume and spatial distribution 
(recall Fig. 2b,c) but the accuracy is still acceptable as the residuals at 3 km resolution for 
Zhengzhou (i) and Shenzhen (j) remain centered and symmetric. Error bars indicate 95% CIs in 
d,e,i,j. The confidence envelopes were generated using 1,000 Monte Carlo simulations. These 
results indicate a relatively robust performance of the proposed ABM against the well-known 
Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) sensitivity tests. The maps in a,f are based on 
administrative boundaries reproduced from MAP WORLD (https://www.tianditu.gov.cn/).

Supplementary Methods 
 
 
Supplementary Method 1 | Model description and configuration 
 
The proposed ABM focuses on the large city-scale human mobility. We study the inflow rather 
than overlapping static population distribution4 so that the model provides a better understanding 
of whether the carrying capacity of a given area can accommodate an influx during flooding 
emergencies. We report the pseudocode of this original model in Supplementary Algorithms 1-9. 
For all individual agents in our experiments, the guiding principle is that action decisions are not 
made in isolation but are shaped by dynamic interplays between risk perceptions, feelings of safety, 
and the observed actions of others5 (included as a proxy for social connections with others and 
subjective norms and attitudes toward collective behavior6). Evacuees’ understanding of the 
environment often translates into a subjective evaluation, which forms the basis of their cognition 
and influences how they assess potential threats or safe areas7. We tune our ABM with the 
following agentic settings: 
 
Each grid is initialized with a predefined population count estimated by 2020-constrained 
WorldPop data (including uninhabited grid identification, the initial number of agents assigned to 
each grid, and grid capacity; see Data availability). At each time step, each agent has the option to 
move in any direction within the Moore neighborhood but is limited to a movement of only one 
grid length per step (averaged moving range of a 5-min travel)8. The capacity 𝐶𝐶𝑖𝑖 of grid 𝑖𝑖 is 
determined by the maximum population of the administrative area where it is located. Based on 
the calibration results, a Gaussian filter with σ  =  1 is adopted for inflow mobility. 
 
The ratio R between the agents with different IGPs for each city is obtained from the China 
Statistical Yearbook 2023 (Supplementary Table 9). Since the yearbook provides statistics only at 
the provincial level, we approximate 𝑅𝑅 for each city using province-level data. To take model 
sensitivity into consideration, we assume that different subgroups of populations, such as local and 
nonlocal residents, have different situational awareness to acquire information due to various 
levels of familiarity with the environment, social ties, access to local media, cultural and linguistic 
understanding, and past experiences (we set different 3×3 and 5×5 grid IGPs for agents; recall Fig. 
1a). 
 
All modeling and simulations were conducted on two AMD EPYC 7T83 64-core processors and 
a GNU/Linux Ubuntu 18.04.6 LTS operation system implemented in Python using PyCharm 
2020.2.3. The system is equipped with 512 GB DDR4 RAM and 16 TB SSD, providing sufficient 
memory and storage to manage the ABM’s dynamic datasets. Note that 1 agent represents 100 
people in the downscaling settings to reduce the computational burden.

Supplementary Method 2 | Estimation of inflow mobility in the ABM

the during-disaster period is July 20–22, 2021, while for the Shenzhen case, it is September 16,

mobile phone signaling data (hourly aggregated Origin-Destination trips for each day at 1 km

Supplementary Method 3 | Evaluation metrics for model performance 
 
We evaluate the performance of proposed ABM along two dimensions: trend and quantity. Each 
dimension consists of a set of multiple metrics. The trend metrics assess whether the model 
correctly reproduces the comparative relationships of spatial pattern of inflows across all grids, 
whereas the quantity metrics evaluate the numerical accuracy between the actual and modeled 
inflow volumes. 
 
We first construct binary indicators for each 1 km grid based on average actual and modeled inflow 
mobility: 
 
𝐵𝐵𝑖𝑖= 𝕀𝕀(𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝑖𝑖> 0), 𝐵𝐵𝚤𝚤෡= 𝕀𝕀(𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝑖𝑖> 0)                              (3)

summarizing true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. A set of trend 
metrics is then defined as:

F1 score.
