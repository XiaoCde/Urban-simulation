### A9. "Share of peers/neighbours who stay vs leave" — published implementations

1. **Borowski & Stathopoulos 2023** (A2): the peer share is survey-elicited, not spatial. Verbatim definition: for all four evacuation alternatives, respondents were told what percentage of their **egocentric social network** selected each alternative; the network consists of **up to five individuals** named by the respondent in a name-generator section. The two model variables are "Share of peers choosing ride" and "Share of peers staying" (design ranges 0–1 and 0–0.8; Table 4 coefficients in A2).
2. **Data-Driven Modeling of Evacuation Decision-Making** (Miami synthetic social network; full text https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10300635 **[index]**): peer influence enters through two thresholds, **cu and cd**, which "control the fraction of neighbors evacuating at which peer-influence for evacuating and for looting, respectively, become important"; the household term ghh dominates the network term gnet in the fitted model.
3. **Arshaghi et al. 2026** (A6): the "peer share" is experimental, not computed: the crowd (5, 15 or 20 NPCs) uniformly takes either the safe or the risky route, so the share following each route is 100%/0% by construction; the participant's route choice and pre-movement time are the outcomes.
4. **Paris high-rise slow-onset flood ABM**, *Journal of Integrated Disaster Risk Management*, DOI 10.5595/001c.18160 **[index]**: social contagion spreads leave-decisions among neighbours within 14 residential high-rise buildings, run probabilistically/Monte Carlo. ### B1. ARR Project 10 — people safety in floods

**Stage 1 (people):** Cox, R. J., Shand, T. D. & Blacka, M. J. (2010), *Appropriate Safety Criteria for People*, Australian Rainfall and Runoff Revision Project 10, Report **P10/S1/006** (also cited as WRL Research Report 240), Water Research Laboratory, UNSW, for the Institution of Engineers Australia. **Stage 2 (vehicles):** Shand, T. (2011), *Appropriate Safety Criteria for Vehicles — Literature Review (Stage 2)*, Report **P10/S2/020**, 21 February 2011, ISBN 978-0-85825-948-5; PDF: http://www.arr-software.org/pdfs/ARR_Project10_Stage2_Vehicle_Stability_Final.pdf **[index]**. **Combined hazard curves:** Smith, G. P., Davey, E. K. & Cox, R. J. (2014), *Flood Hazard*, WRL Technical Report **2014/07**, UNSW — the source of the H1–H6 classification adopted in ARR 2019 Book 6.

**Hazard classification verbatim** (Smith et al. 2014, as reproduced identically in multiple citing sources): H1: D·V ≤ 0.3, limiting depth D = 0.3 m, limiting velocity V = 2.0 m/s — "Generally safe for vehicles, people and buildings"; H2: D·V ≤ 0.6, D = 0.5 m, V = 2.0 m/s — "Unsafe for small vehicles"; H3: D·V ≤ 0.6, D = 1.2 m, V = 2.0 m/s — "Unsafe for vehicles, children and the elderly"; H4: D·V ≤ 1.0, D = 2.0 m, V = 2.0 m/s — "Unsafe for vehicles and people"; H5: D·V ≤ 4.0, D = 4.0 m, V = 4.0 m/s — "Unsafe for vehicles and people. All buildings subject to structural damage…"; H6: D·V > 4.0 — "Unsafe for vehicles and people. All building types considered vulnerable to failure".

**Verification of the model's hand-set depths.** The model's "adult 1.2 m" coincides with the **H3 limiting still-water depth** and with older Australian guidance quoted verbatim in the ARR Project 10 presentation (Smith, WRL; https://arr.ga.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/40488/ARR_People_and_Vehicles_stability.pdf **[index]**): "EMA advice is that wading by adults becomes difficult and dangerous when the depth of still water exceeds 1.2 m or when the velocity of shallow water exceeds 0.8 m/s and for various combinations of DxV between these limits" (attributed there to DPW 1986). The model's "child 0.5 m" coincides with the **H2 limiting depth**. Neither number is an experimentally measured individual instability depth: both are classification limits of a hazard-curve system. The same presentation states the underlying criteria verbatim: "The current AR&R guideline of D*V=0.4 m²/s is suitable for children (H*M 25 to 50 mKg) BUT conservative for healthy adults who if trained could be expected to work in conditions up to D*V= 0.8 m²/s. Extreme hazard is indicated for all adults at D*V > 1.2 m²/s." Experimental basis cited in Project 10: Foster & Cox (1973) flume exposure testing of **9- and 13-year-old** children (small, preliminary laboratory work), plus the literature synthesis in Cox, Shand & Blacka (2010); Shand et al. also record the D·V ≤ 0.4 m²/s criterion as ensuring "a low hazard for children, providing that the maximum depth is limited to 0.5 m and the maximum velocity to 3.0 m/s". Vehicle interim criteria (Stage 2): floating limits 0.3 m (small passenger cars), 0.4 m (large passenger cars), 0.5 m (4WD), all stability criteria with a limiting velocity of 3.0 m/s.

### B2. DEFRA / Environment Agency FD2321 and FD2320 — hazard rating

**Clarifying document:** "Supplementary Note on Flood Hazard Ratings and Thresholds for Development Planning and Control Purpose", Environment Agency / HR Wallingford, May 2008 (clarifies FD2321/TR1 p.10 §3.5 and FD2320/TR2 Table 13.1). Copy read: https://docs.planning.org.uk/20210521/28/QTGCTMTY0PT00/ze3hnpzxd6iubjvc.pdf **[full text read]**.

**Formula verbatim:** HR = d × (v + n) + DF, presented in the note as **HR = d × (v + 0.5) + DF** — i.e. the "n" is **0.5** — where d = depth of flooding (m), v = velocity of floodwater (m/s), DF = debris factor (0, 0.5 or 1 depending on the probability that debris will lead to a significantly greater hazard).

**Debris factors, FD2321/TR1 Table 3.1 verbatim:** depths 0–0.25 m: Pasture/Arable 0, Woodland 0, Urban 0; depths 0.25–0.75 m: 0, 0.5, 1; d > 0.75 m and/or v > 2: 0.5, 1, 1. **FD2320/TR2 Table 13.1 instead applied** DF = 0.5 for depths ≤ 0.25 m and DF = 1.0 for depths > 0.25 m.

**Bands — which document owns which numbers:** FD2321 Table 3.2: HR < 0.75 Low ("Caution"); 0.75–1.25 Moderate ("Dangerous for some (i.e. children)"); 1.25–2.5 Significant ("Dangerous for most people"); > 2.5 Extreme ("Dangerous for all"). **FD2320** lowered the danger-for-all threshold from 2.5 to **2.0** as a precaution (Project Board decision). Therefore the commonly cited **0.75 / 1.25 / 2.0** band set is **FD2320's**; FD2321's own top threshold is 2.5.
